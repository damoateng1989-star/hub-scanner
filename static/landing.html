How it works

Three steps. One clear plan.

01

A hub forms

4-hour closes hold inside a tight range for at least 5 sessions. The stock is coiling, and there's no trade yet.

02

A candle closes outside

The first 4-hour close beyond the hub is the breakout, never the wick. On Pro you're warned about 15 minutes before it prints.

03

It travels to the shelf

Earlier hubs act as shelves. The nearest one is the target; a close back inside the hub means it failed. Entry, target and exit, set before you enter.

From hub to alert, on your phone.

Hubs, start to finish: the warning before the close, the confirmation on the close, the target when it hits. Most days the scanner says nothing. That's the discipline, built in.     FAQ

    Questions, answered.

    What is a hub?

    A stretch of at least 5 sessions where every 4-hour candle closes inside a tight range, measured against the stock's own volatility (ATR). It's the stock coiling before a move, and while price is inside, there's no trade.

    What is a shelf?

    An earlier hub. When price breaks out of the current hub, the nearest earlier hub in that direction is where it often heads next, so it becomes the target.

    I have a job. Can I really use this?

    That's who it's for. The 4-hour candles close at 1:30 PM and 4:00 PM ET, so there are only two decision points a day. You get a morning brief before work, alerts on your phone only when a candle closes outside a hub, and an evening recap after the close.

    When do alerts arrive?

    Pre-close warnings (Pro) come about 15 minutes before each 4-hour close, at 1:15 and 3:45 PM ET. For the 1:30 PM close you get an early read at about 1:31 PM from the real-time feed, confirmed (or corrected) at about 1:47 PM with the full market data, so you can still enter that afternoon. The 4:00 PM close is confirmed after the market shuts, and you can choose to skip those alerts in Settings. Regular trading hours only.

    Which setups does NextShelf find?

    Hubs are the core setup. NextShelf also finds bull and bear flags, double tops and bottoms, triangles, channels, and support and resistance tested 3 or more times. They follow the same rule: act on a 4-hour close outside the pattern, aim for the next shelf. Each keeps its own public track record.

    Which stocks are covered?

    Every S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 stock plus major ETFs, over 540 in all. You can also add any US-listed ticker to your own watchlist.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. NextShelf is an educational tool describing past price behavior. Signals fail. Do your own research and never risk money you can't afford to lose.
