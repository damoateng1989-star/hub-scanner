How it works

Three steps. One clear plan.

A simple, repeatable setup on 4-hour candles, with every rule spelled out so you always know why an alert fired.

1

A hub forms

4H closes hold inside a tight range for at least 4 sessions. The stock is coiling.

2

A candle closes outside

The first 4H close above or below the hub is the breakout. You're warned 15 minutes before it prints.

3

It travels to the next shelf

Earlier hubs act as shelves. The nearest one is the target, and a close back inside the hub means it failed.

    FAQ

    Questions, answered.

    What is a hub?

    A stretch of at least 4 sessions where every 4-hour candle closes inside a tight range, measured against the stock's own volatility (ATR). It's the stock coiling before a move.

    What is a shelf?

    An earlier hub. When price breaks out of the current hub, the nearest earlier hub in that direction is where it often heads next, so it becomes the target.

    When do alerts arrive?

    Pre-close warnings (Pro) come about 15 minutes before each 4H close, at 1:15 and 3:45 PM ET. Breakout alerts follow shortly after the candle closes. Regular trading hours only.

    Which setups does NextShelf find?

    Hubs are the core setup. For other trading styles, NextShelf also finds bull and bear flags, double tops and bottoms, symmetrical triangles, ascending and descending channels, and support and resistance levels tested 3 or more times. They follow the same rules: act on a 4-hour close outside the pattern and aim for the next shelf. Pick the ones you trade in Settings, and each setup keeps its own public track record so you can see which actually works.

    Which stocks are covered?

    The board scans every S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 stock plus major ETFs (SPY, QQQ, sector funds and more), over 540 in all. You can also add any US-listed ticker to your own watchlist.

    I have a job. Can I really use this?

    That's who it's for. The 4-hour candles close at 1:30 PM and 4:00 PM ET, so there are only two decision points a day. You get a morning brief before work, alerts on your phone only when a candle closes outside a hub, and an evening recap after the close with each breakout's target and exit level.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. NextShelf is an educational tool that describes past price behaviour. Signals can and do fail. Do your own research and never risk money you can't afford to lose.
