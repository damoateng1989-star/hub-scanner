How it works

Three steps. One clear plan.

01

A hub forms

4-hour closes hold inside a tight range for at least 4 four-hour candles. The stock is coiling, and there's no trade yet.

02

A candle closes outside

The first 4-hour close beyond the hub is the breakout, never the wick. On Pro you're warned about 15 minutes before it prints.

03

It travels to the shelf

Earlier hubs act as shelves. The nearest one is the target; a close back inside the hub means it failed. Entry, target and exit, set before you enter.

From hub to alert, on your phone.

    FAQ

    Questions, answered.

    What is a hub?

    A stretch of at least 4 four-hour candles where every 4-hour candle closes inside a tight range, measured against the stock's own volatility (ATR). It's the stock coiling before a move, and while price is inside, there's no trade.

    What is a shelf?

    An earlier hub. When price breaks out of the current hub, the nearest earlier hub in that direction is where it often heads next, so it becomes the target.

    How does a trade play out after a breakout?

    The aim is the next shelf in the direction of the breakout. If there is no shelf ahead, the aim is one ATR beyond the hub. The trade ends when price reaches the aim, when a 4-hour candle closes back inside the hub, or after 10 candles, whichever comes first. NextShelf's paper trades follow the same rules, and also take profit early at a gamma wall that sits before the shelf.

    I have a job. Can I really use this?

    That's who it's for. The 4-hour candles close at 1:30 PM and 4:00 PM ET, so there are only two decision points a day. You get a morning brief before work, alerts on your phone only when a candle closes outside a hub, and an evening recap after the close.

    When do alerts arrive?

    Every alert goes to Telegram. On Pro you can also receive a text for every alert, and a phone call for pre-close warnings and breakouts. Pre-close warnings (Pro) come about 15 minutes before each 4-hour close, at 1:15 and 3:45 PM ET. For the 1:30 PM close you get an early read at about 1:31 PM from the real-time feed, confirmed (or corrected) at about 1:47 PM with the full market data, so you can still enter that afternoon. The 4:00 PM close is confirmed after the market shuts, and you can choose to skip those alerts in Settings. Regular trading hours only.

    Which setups does NextShelf find?

    Hubs are the core setup. NextShelf also finds bull and bear flags, double tops and bottoms, triangles, channels, and support and resistance tested 3 or more times. They follow the same rule: act on a 4-hour close outside the pattern, aim for the next shelf. The hub track record is public below; the other patterns are in beta and their records publish here as they fill in.

    Does NextShelf check gamma walls?

    Yes, on confirmed breakouts. NextShelf finds the strike with the most options gamma stacked near the price, from the Cboe delayed options chain, refreshed daily. If that wall sits between the breakout and the next shelf, the Shelf card and the alert flag it, because dealer hedging tends to slow price there. It is a flag, not a veto: the setup still counts.

    Does NextShelf trade its own setups?

    On paper only. NextShelf paper-trades one setup a day in a paper account, with no real money, to test its rules in real time. The public track record above counts every hub setup NextShelf logs, not just the paper trades. Members can open any setup in Webull or Robinhood straight from the setups list or the chart.

    Which stocks are covered?

    Every S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 stock plus major ETFs, over 540 in all. You can also add any US-listed ticker to your own watchlist.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. NextShelf is an educational tool describing past price behavior. Setups fail. Do your own research and never risk money you can't afford to lose.
